What are “books of accounts”?Ano ang “books of accounts”?

Books of accounts are your business’s organized record of every peso that comes in and goes out. Think of them as your business diary — but written in a standard way, so anyone (you, an accountant, or the BIR) can follow the money from start to finish.

Ang books of accounts ay ang organisadong listahan ng bawat pisong pumapasok at lumalabas sa negosyo mo. Parang diary ng negosyo mo — pero nakasulat sa standard na paraan, para masundan ng kahit sino (ikaw, accountant, o ang BIR) kung saan galing at saan napunta ang pera.

The two main kinds of books are journals (everything in date order) and ledgers (everything sorted by account, like “Cash” or “Internet Expense”). You’ll learn both in Module 3.

Dalawang pangunahing uri ng libro: ang journal (lahat ng transaksyon ayon sa petsa) at ang ledger (lahat ay naka-grupo ayon sa account, gaya ng “Cash” o “Internet Expense”). Pag-aaralan natin pareho sa Module 3.

Do I really need them?Kailangan ko ba talaga ito?

Yes. Every business taxpayer, including self-employed professionals and freelancers, must keep books of accounts.

Oo. Lahat ng business taxpayer, kasama ang mga self-employed professional at freelancer, ay kailangang mag-ingat ng books of accounts.

Why does the BIR care? Your tax return is built from your income and expenses. Your books are where those numbers come from, and your documents (invoices, receipts) are the proof behind each number. Good books mean you can show exactly how you got the amounts on your return.

Bakit mahalaga ito sa BIR? Ang tax return mo ay galing sa kita at gastos mo. Sa libro mo nanggagaling ang mga numerong iyon, at ang mga dokumento mo (invoice, resibo) ang patunay sa likod ng bawat numero. Kapag maayos ang libro mo, kaya mong ipakita kung paano mo nakuha ang mga halaga sa return mo.

Every peso follows the same 6-step path. Here it is, with an example from Ana, a fictional freelance graphic designer you’ll meet in the practice lab.

💡 Why it mattersBakit ito mahalaga?

  • Your tax return is only as good as the numbers behind it. Books are where those numbers live.
  • Recording as you go takes minutes. Rebuilding a whole quarter from a pile of receipts takes days.
  • Clear books show you whether your business is actually earning — useful even beyond taxes.
  • Kasing-ayos lang ng tax return mo ang mga numerong pinanggalingan nito. Nasa libro ang mga numerong iyon.
  • Ilang minuto lang kung regular kang nagre-record. Pero ilang araw kung bubuuin mo ulit ang buong quarter mula sa tambak ng resibo.
  • Kapag malinaw ang libro, makikita mo kung kumikita ba talaga ang negosyo mo — kapaki-pakinabang kahit hindi para sa tax.

⚠️ Common mistakesMga karaniwang pagkakamali

  • “I’m just a freelancer, I don’t need books.” Freelancers are business taxpayers too.
  • Mixing personal and business money in one wallet or account, so you can’t tell what belongs to the business.
  • Waiting until the week of filing to record three months of transactions.
  • Throwing away receipts and invoices because “it’s already recorded.” The document is the proof.
  • Keeping one set of books “for the BIR” and another “real” one. Only one set is allowed.
  • “Freelancer lang naman ako, hindi ko kailangan ng libro.” Business taxpayer din ang mga freelancer.
  • Paghahalo ng personal at business na pera sa iisang wallet o account, kaya hindi mo alam kung alin ang sa negosyo.
  • Hinihintay ang linggo ng filing bago i-record ang tatlong buwan ng transaksyon.
  • Pagtapon ng resibo at invoice dahil “naka-record na naman.” Ang dokumento ang patunay.
  • Isang libro “para sa BIR” at isa pang “totoo.” Isang set lang ang pinapayagan.
