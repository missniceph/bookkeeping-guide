Recording transactionsPag-record ng mga transaksyon

Debit and credit are the part everyone fears. Good news: it’s just a sorting rule. Learn one table and you’re set.

Ang debit at credit ang pinakakinatatakutan ng lahat. Good news: patakaran lang ito sa pag-aayos. Kabisaduhin ang isang table at ayos ka na.

Double entry: every peso comes from somewhere and goes somewhereDouble entry: bawat piso ay may pinanggalingan at pinuntahan

When you pay your internet bill, two things happen at once: your cash goes down, and your internet expense goes up. Double-entry bookkeeping records both sides of every transaction. That’s why every entry touches at least two accounts, and why total debits always equal total credits.

Kapag binayaran mo ang internet bill, dalawang bagay ang sabay na nangyayari: bumababa ang cash mo, at tumataas ang gastos mo sa internet. Sa double-entry bookkeeping, nire-record ang dalawang panig ng bawat transaksyon. Kaya bawat entry ay may hindi bababa sa dalawang account, at laging pantay ang kabuuang debit at kabuuang credit.

Debit and credit: the one table to rememberDebit at credit: ang isang table na dapat tandaan

Debit just means the left column. Credit means the right column. They are not “good” and “bad,” and credit doesn’t mean “money for you.” Each type of account goes up on one side and down on the other:

Ang debit ay ang kaliwang column lang. Ang credit ay ang kanang column. Hindi ibig sabihin ay “maganda” o “masama,” at hindi rin “pera para sa iyo” ang credit. Bawat uri ng account ay tumataas sa isang side at bumababa sa kabila:

Memory trick: “DEA-LER.” Drawings, Expenses, Assets go up with a debit. Liabilities, Equity, Revenue go up with a credit.

Pantanda: “DEA-LER.” Ang Drawings, Expenses, Assets ay tumataas sa debit. Ang Liabilities, Equity, Revenue ay tumataas sa credit.

Cash basis vs accrual: when do you record it?Cash basis vs accrual: kailan mo ito ire-record?

💵 Cash basisCash basis

Record income when you receive the money, and expenses when you pay. Simple, and it matches your bank account.

I-record ang kita kapag natanggap mo na ang pera, at ang gastos kapag nabayaran mo na. Simple, at tugma sa bank account mo.

🧾 Accrual basisAccrual basis

Record income when you earn it (the work is done and billed), and expenses when you incur them — even if no money has moved yet.

I-record ang kita kapag kinita mo na (tapos na ang trabaho at na-bill na), at ang gastos kapag nagkaroon ka na nito — kahit wala pang perang gumagalaw.

Example: Ana finishes a project and bills ₱6,000 on Oct 28. The client pays on Nov 5.

Halimbawa: Natapos ni Ana ang project at nag-bill ng ₱6,000 noong Okt 28. Nagbayad ang client noong Nob 5.

Under cash basis the income lands in November; under accrual it lands in October. Same peso, different month.

Sa cash basis, sa Nobyembre napupunta ang kita; sa accrual, sa Oktubre. Parehong piso, ibang buwan.

Choose one method and apply it consistently. Don’t switch back and forth between months — your totals won’t be comparable, and income could get counted twice or not at all. If you’re unsure which method fits you, ask your RDO or an accountant. The practice lab uses the cash basis.

Pumili ng isang paraan at gamitin ito nang tuloy-tuloy. Huwag magpalipat-lipat bawat buwan — hindi na maikukumpara ang mga total mo, at pwedeng madoble o hindi mabilang ang kita. Kung hindi ka sigurado kung alin ang bagay sa iyo, magtanong sa RDO mo o sa accountant. Sa practice lab, cash basis ang gamit.

💡 Why it mattersBakit ito mahalaga?

  • Double entry has a built-in error check: if debits don’t equal credits, something is wrong.
  • Putting things in the right account (expense vs asset vs drawings) changes your income and expense totals.
  • A consistent method means each month’s numbers mean the same thing.
  • May built-in na error check ang double entry: kapag hindi pantay ang debit at credit, may mali.
  • Ang paglagay sa tamang account (expense vs asset vs drawings) ay nakakaapekto sa total ng kita at gastos mo.
  • Kapag consistent ang paraan, pareho ang ibig sabihin ng mga numero bawat buwan.

⚠️ Common mistakesMga karaniwang pagkakamali

  • Thinking “debit = minus, credit = plus.” It depends on the account type.
  • Recording only one side (e.g., writing the income but not the cash).
  • Recording your own money put into the business as income. It’s capital.
  • Recording personal spending as a business expense. It’s drawings.
  • Recording equipment that lasts for years as this month’s expense.
  • Switching between cash and accrual from month to month.
  • Akalang “debit = bawas, credit = dagdag.” Depende ito sa uri ng account.
  • Isang side lang ang nire-record (hal. isinulat ang kita pero hindi ang cash).
  • Itinuturing na kita ang sarili mong perang ipinasok sa negosyo. Capital iyon.
  • Itinuturing na business expense ang personal na gastos. Drawings iyon.
  • Itinuturing na gastos ngayong buwan ang equipment na ilang taon gagamitin.
  • Pagpapalipat-lipat sa cash at accrual bawat buwan.
